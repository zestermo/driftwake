# Inspirations: what Driftwake can borrow (2026-10-26)

Driftwake = **Pirate Warriors 4** (feel strong, crew rushes, big spectacle) + **Risk of Rain 2** (a run that
escalates, builds that snowball, 1-4 co-op on one economy) + **Xenoverse 2** (a custom captain, moves learned from
masters, a loadout you build, replayable missions) + **soulslike** fights (poise, telegraphs, punish windows) +
**co-op/party RPGs** (roles, combos between players). Notes on each, then suggestions mapped onto Driftwake and 1.23.
Web sources were thin (guides and forums). Where a detail comes from general knowledge of the games, it's marked *(k)*.

## What each game does that matters here

### One Piece: Pirate Warriors 4
- **Bosses have a guard gauge** that blocks knockback until it's broken; then they can be stunned and juggled. That's
  the poise meter 1.23 decided, and the proof it works in exactly this genre.
- **A 4-move special loadout** picked before a mission from a bigger list per character, plus a Full Force/"awakening"
  state (Gear Fourth) unlocked by story and equipped like a move.
- **Character types** (Power, Speed, Technique, Sky *(k)*): each type has its own dash, air game and burst bonus. A
  family identity on top of each character's moves.
- **Kizuna Rush** *(PW3, carried over (k))*: a gauge filled by fighting calls crewmates in for combination attacks; the
  more who join, the bigger the finisher.
- **Destructible battlefields**: buildings collapse, dust and smoke fill the screen. Spectacle comes from the place,
  not only the move.
- **Growth Map**: a skill board bought with berries and coins earned mostly from the story; the hardest Treasure Log
  challenges give the rarest coins.
- **Musou crowds** *(k)*: lots of weak foes to feel strong, a few officers with guard gauges to fight properly.

### Risk of Rain 2
- **Difficulty rises with time**: a HUD bar; enemies grow stronger and more numerous the longer you take. Pressure to
  keep moving, and the stakes keep rising.
- **The director** *(k)*: spawns are bought with credits that grow with time and difficulty, so the mix and the elites
  adapt to how strong the run is.
- **Elites** *(k)*: any enemy can roll an affix (blazing, glacial, overloading, malachite...) with its own visuals and
  threat.
- **Items stack with no cap**: every copy compounds, and builds snowball through synergies between items.
- **Shrines**: optional risk-for-reward (blood: HP for gold; chance: gamble; combat: summon a harder fight for loot;
  order: reroll your whole inventory).
- **The teleporter event**: start it and the island fights back (a boss and a wave while it charges), then you move on.
- **Lunar items**: powerful with a real downside; swingy and sometimes run-ending, which some players love.
- **Artifacts** *(k)*: run modifiers (no healing, enemies drop items, everyone shares one skill...).

### Dragon Ball Xenoverse 2
- **A custom character's loadout**: 4 supers + 2 ultimates + an evasive + an **awoken skill** (a transformation that
  changes stats and sometimes the basic attacks), plus **Super Souls** (passives).
- **Mentors**: famous fighters in the hub teach their moves. Pass their test to become their student, train with them,
  bring them on quests, and they teach more as the bond grows.
- **Parallel Quests**: ~100 replayable missions rated 1-7 stars, co-op with up to three, dropping skills and gear.
  **Hidden "ultimate finish" conditions** (beat a boss a certain way) give special rewards.
- **Expert missions and raids**: big co-op fights for the hardest skills.
- **Story-gated awakenings**: some transformations unlock only at saga moments.

### Soulslikes
- **Poise / posture**: heavy and charged hits build stagger faster; a broken stance opens a critical. Bosses have
  more poise, and it recovers faster while they're attacking.
- **Telegraphs + punish windows**: every big attack has a readable wind-up and a punishable recovery. The criticism
  of Elden Ring is delays long enough that dodging on the tell is too early; Driftwake should stay readable, not
  "catch you".
- *(k)* Checkpoints (bonfires), limited heals (estus), dropping what you carry on death and getting it back,
  bosses with phase changes, enemies that read your healing and punish it.

### Co-op and party RPGs (Monster Hunter, Hades, Ember Knights)
- **Monster Hunter part breaks**: breaking a part guarantees a flinch, can knock down or topple the monster, **changes
  its attacks** (a broken arm makes it slide, a cut tail shortens a sweep) and gives extra materials. Hitting legs
  trips it, the head stuns it, blunt builds stun. Co-op friendly fire that flinches teammates is a known annoyance.
- **Hades**: **Duo boons** appear once you hold boons from two gods; keepsakes raise the odds; the Mirror of Night is
  permanent meta progression. *(k)* **The Pact of Punishment / Heat**: you choose to make runs harder for better
  rewards.
- **Ember Knights**: each weapon has a battlefield job (crowd control, ranged, burst) so a party wants different
  kits; encounters are built so all four stay on screen together.

## Suggestions for Driftwake

Each is tagged with where it fits: **[1.23]** strengthens a 1.23 decision, **[new]** is a new idea for a later grill.

### Combat
1. **[1.23] Guard gauge on bosses and officers, shown on screen** (PW4). The 1.23 poise meter becomes visible for big
   foes: a bar under the boss's HP. Grunts' poise stays hidden. When it breaks: a big stagger (the beast pass's stagger
   pose), a few seconds of bonus damage, a juggle window, a camera punch. Knockdown moves become *how you break
   poise faster*, not a free stun.
2. **[1.23] Poise damage by move, not just damage** (soulslike): kicks and heavies chip poise hard, light combos barely.
   Black leg becomes the poise-breaker style, the daggers the bleed and crit style: each weapon family gets a job
   (Ember Knights).
3. **[1.23] Part breaks on beast bosses** (Monster Hunter): 2-3 breakable parts each (the Silverback's tusks, the
   scorpion's stinger, the sand worm's armour plates). A break staggers it, **changes a move** (no stinger: no poison
   jab), and drops a part material for crafting. This gives co-op crews something to coordinate ("go for the tail!").
4. **[1.23] Elites with affixes** (RoR2): any grunt or beast can roll Blazing, Frozen, Stormcharged, Iron, Venom or
   Ghostly, each with a look (a tinted aura) and a threat. More often on later islands and at higher bounty. Already
   decided as "elites and mixes"; this makes it a shared system on every enemy.
5. **[new] Crew Rush** (PW Kizuna Rush + Hades Duo boons): a crew gauge filled by fighting near each other. Spend it
   and the captains who join do a **combination finisher** based on their two styles (black leg + katana: a flaming
   cross-cut; two pistols: a crossfire). Solo: a smaller "Captain's Rush". The co-op hook that makes playing together
   feel like a One Piece crew.
6. **[new] Fighting-style families with their own movement** (PW4 types): Power (axe, hammer, greatsword: armoured
   dash, poise breaking), Speed (black leg, daggers, dual: a longer dash, air combos), Technique (katana, cutlass,
   polearm: counters, parries), Ranged (guns). Each family gets a signature dash or air move, so switching loadouts
   changes how you move, not just your attacks.
7. **[new] Awakening per tree** (XV2 awoken skill + PW Gear Fourth): the ultimate tier becomes a timed transformation
   (Red-Hot Leg's heat state: new combo string, faster kicks, fire on every hit) rather than one big hit. The drawn
   "awakening" FX series already points this way.

### Progression
8. **[1.23] Mentors and trainers** (XV2): the per-tree points decided in 1.23 get a face. Each weapon tree has a
   **master** somewhere in the chain (the black-leg chef already teaches it). Meet them, pass their test (a duel on
   the captain AI), and they teach the tree's techniques as your mastery rises, with a short "learned!" training
   scene. A tree's last nodes need the master's final test. That's discovery plus a reason to visit places.
9. **[1.23] A loadout screen that reads like XV2's**: 5 ability slots (already 1-5), 3 passives (already
   `PASSIVE_SLOTS` 3), 1 ultimate/awakening, and the weapon loadouts. One clear screen showing what's equipped and
   what's learned but not slotted. That fixes "I only had two abilities" whether it was points or visibility.
10. **[new] Hidden finishes** (XV2 ultimate finish): each arc villain and boss has 1-2 secret conditions (beat the
   tyrant without killing his men, break the stinger first, finish with a Crew Rush) that give a special reward: a
   unique trait weapon, a technique variant, a bounty bump. Replay value, and talk between players.
11. **[new] Shrines as island finds** (RoR2): the "small finds" decided in 1.20, with stakes. A **blood shrine** (pay
   HP for gold), a **gambler's idol** (pay gold, maybe a trait weapon), a **challenge totem** (summon an elite fight
   for a better chest), a **sea god's altar** (trade a weapon for a random one a tier up). Cheap to build, big in play.
12. **[new] Cursed items** (RoR2 lunar): rare purple/gold relics with a strong upside and a real downside (a Driftfruit
   echo: +40% damage, but you sink like a fruit user; a cursed compass: always finds treasure, draws the Admiralty).
   They fit the fog isle and the treasure economy.

### Run structure and pressure
13. **[1.23] Bounty as heat** (RoR2 time scaling + Hades Pact): the bounty is the game's difficulty bar, shown in the
   1.23 Wanted tab and HUD. A higher bounty means more and stronger patrols, elites more often, hunters, better loot
   and prices at shady shops. You choose to raise it (sink Marine ships, hidden finishes) or lie low. That ties all of
   1.23's sea and Marine decisions to one number you understand.
14. **[1.23] The arc's showdown as a teleporter event** (RoR2): starting the villain's showdown makes the island answer
   (his crew pours in, the alarm bell rings, the Marines' blockade starts forming at sea). Then the post-boss Marine
   ring from 1.23 is the natural next beat.
15. **[new] Sea Charts as Parallel Quests** (XV2 PQs + PW4 Treasure Log): replayable, star-rated challenge missions bought
   or found as charts (raid a Marine convoy, hold a village for 3 minutes, a duel with a master, a boss rematch with a
   twist), co-op for the crew. The best rewards (masters' final techniques, awakenings, rare emblems) come from 5-7
   star charts. This is post-game and between-arcs content that doesn't need a new island.
16. **[new] Death with stakes, not punishment** (soulslike): when every captain is down, you drop your unbanked loot and
   some gold where you fell (a marker; the arc's enemies guard it). Get it back or lose it. Arrest by the Admiralty
   (1.19) stays the alternative when they did it.

### Spectacle
17. **[1.23] Breakable places** (PW4): crates, stalls, fences, palisades and market awnings break under heavy hits and
   ultimates (debris on a timer, PS1-cheap). Boss arenas get a few big breakables (a pillar the scorpion knocks down,
   which then blocks a line). Fits the beast spectacle pass.
18. **[new] Musou moments** (PW4): some arc beats throw a crowd at you (20-30 weak grunts on cheap LOD bodies, one hit
   each) with an officer to fight properly. Feeling overpowered for a minute is part of shonen fantasy, and it's the
   contrast that makes poise fights land.

## If only five go into 1.23
1. Visible guard gauge on bosses and officers, poise damage by move (1, 2)
2. Part breaks on beast bosses (3)
3. Mentors teaching each tree, with the clear loadout screen (8, 9)
4. Elites with affixes (4)
5. Bounty as heat (13)
