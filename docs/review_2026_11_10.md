# Project review, 2026-11-10: performance plan, bugs, what next

A read-only pass over the code, git history, docs, the site, the bench results and the play sessions' perf logs
(`user://perf*.log`), plus one bench pair run today. Nothing in the game was changed. Line numbers are as of 5850f91a.
Two background audits (a bug hunt, a per-frame code audit) did the wide reading; their top findings were re-read in
the code before they went in here. "Not verified" marks what is inferred.

## 1. Where the project is

- 2,057 commits and 43 tagged releases in five weeks (v1.8 on 10-09 to v1.30.3 on 11-07): 234k lines of GDScript in
  677 scripts. Every release added systems; none since v1.14 was about speed.
- The perf gate's baseline is from 10-24 and was never reset; the last three releases went out without it meaning
  anything. The frame grew in exactly that window (below).
- Playtests (11-02 four captains, 11-07 two) say the same two things: the host drops under 30 fps in fights and in
  some places, and players use the main combat path and little else (fishing never, 0 treasure maps, arena rank 0,
  734 gold unspent, katana mastery 18 and everything else 1-2).

## 2. Performance

### What the numbers say

Brinehollow bench on the min-spec PC itself (Ryzen 5 5600G + RTX 3080, 1080p, editor debug, solo, nobody fighting).
The mean of the plaza and market stops, the same two in every column (runs from different days: +-10%; the 1.30.2
run was on a slower day, calib 26.6 against ~24.7):

| | 10-24 baseline | v1.29 (11-02) | v1.30.2 (11-05) | v1.30.3 (11-08) | v1.31 (today) | 1.30.3 + one guest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean frame | 14.6 ms | 14.8 | 19.3 | 17.3 | 17.0 | 21.4 |
| process scripts | - | 5.1 | 6.5 | 6.2 | 6.2 | 8.0 |
| draw (main thread) | - | 5.4 | 6.4 | 5.6 | 5.4 | 6.9 |
| tail (transform notifications) | - | 2.4 | 3.3 | 2.8 | 2.8 | 3.3 |
| shadow draws | 720 | 714 | 723 | 759 | 764 | 781 |
| nodes | - | 37.7k | 41.7k | 41.7k | 41.8k | 41.8k |
| physics ticks a frame | 0.88 | 0.89 | 1.16 | 1.04 | 1.02 | 1.28 |

- **A calm town is over the 16.7 ms budget**, before a fight, a storm or a guest. Past 16.7 ms the engine runs more
  than one physics tick a frame, so every extra ms costs more than a ms. That is why fights fall off a cliff: the
  11-07 host log has 50 ms frames at 3 ticks a frame with 20 enemies awake.
- **The game is CPU-bound on the main thread; the GPU is idle** (2-4 ms). Scripts are under half the frame. Draw
  submission plus tail (8.2 ms) is bigger than the process scripts (6.2 ms), which is why trimming script by script
  has felt like diminishing returns.
- **There was never headroom, and 1.30 used what there was.** The town sat at 14.6-14.8 ms from 10-24 to v1.29; 1.30
  added ~2.2 ms (scripts +1.1, 4k nodes, the ticks that follow). 1.30.3's clothing and 1.31's feel work cost little
  on this bench. So the job is the standing costs (draws, shadows, rigs, the loaded world), not one bad commit.
- **The world is all loaded all the time:** 37-42k nodes, 227-268 enemies, 21k collision shapes, with two chain
  islands 2 km away fully built (25k of those nodes: the 11-09 log goes 14k to 39k nodes in the first 90 s).
- **Play-session logs** (RTX 3080, release build, 11-07, 93 min hosting one guest): 30-second windows at 23-34 fps
  in fights and on arrival at islands; the log's own timers (anim, ai) explain 1-5 ms of 25-50 ms frames. The solo
  editor session on 11-09 held 55-60 fps in Brinehollow with 1% lows of 36-42.
- `CLAUDE.md`'s target ("the laptop's bench at <= 10 ms") is stale: benches now run on the min-spec PC. The bar
  proposed here: **town stops <= 12 ms on this PC in the editor build** (ticks under 0.8, headroom for a fight and a
  guest), and **no stop over 16 ms**.

### Tested today: a separate render thread is not an option

The idea: move the 5-7 ms of draw submission off the main thread with `--render-thread separate` (one setting).
Bench pair, back to back: the normal run gave the v1.31 column above; the separate-thread run never reached the
first stop. Godot 4.7.2 itself warns the mode is experimental; the game then tripped "buffer_update can only be
called from the render thread", a `multimesh_instance_get_transform` sync every frame (`draw_lod.gd:91`), image
update errors and an endless `self_list` error flood. **Ruled out** until the engine calls it stable and those call
sites are fixed. The draw cost has to come down by drawing less. (The flood wrote a 4.8 GB log and filled C: for a
few minutes; it's moved to `D:\driftwake_scratch\brinehollow_rt_sep.log` and can be deleted. bench.ps1 has no log
cap like the test runner's 25 MB: worth adding.)

### What the draw is made of (measured 2026-11-10, after the shadow test)

The bench has a `setup` column now (`RenderingServer.get_frame_setup_time_cpu`: moved and changed instances and canvas
items brought up to date before any viewport renders) and prints each SubViewport's render time per stop.

| ms a frame | plaza | market | melee |
|---|---|---|---|
| draw (main thread, whole) | 5.27 | 4.73 | 6.52 |
| setup (dirty instances + canvas) | 0.83 | 0.75 | 0.73 |
| rcpu (the root viewport's render: cull, lists, shadows, post) | 2.34 | 2.07 | 3.08 |
| the seven 960x540 SubViewports (HUD 0.12, six closed wheels and the chat ~0.01 each, all UPDATE_ALWAYS) | ~0.2 | ~0.2 | - |
| not attributed (particles, canvas render, the frame's end and submit) | ~1.9 | ~1.7 | ~2.7 |

So about 2 ms of the draw can't be attributed from scripts: the engine's frame profiler isn't exposed to GDScript
(`get_frame_profile` is editor-only in 4.7). Getting further needs a native profiler (Tracy, Superluminal or VTune on
the release template) for one session. Until then the draw's known levers are small: fewer rigs posed a frame (their
part meshes are the dirty instances), fewer surfaces per body, and nothing from shadows.

**The order by what is measured,** biggest first: (a) rigs: posing and skinning are 3.3 ms of the town's 6.2 ms of
scripts and grow in a fight (plan item 4, then 2); (b) a fight's physics scripts: 3.65 ms a tick against 1.3 in town,
6.4-8.5 ms a frame (phase 2); (c) the loaded world: ~1.9 ms of far scripts and half the nodes (phase 3); (d) the
island let-go minute (item 3); (e) the small ungated scripts (item 5).

### The plan

**Phase 0, done 2026-11-10** (dev_notes "perf phase 0"). The bench already had `brawl`, `isles` (an island let go
and rebuilt) and `coop`; what was missing is in: the **`melee` stop** (20 grunts awake), the **frame's split in
perf.log**, a **draw-count fail in the gate**, a 64 MB cap on the bench log. First melee numbers (v1.31, editor build):
solo 29.0 ms mean (79 frames over 33 ms, 1.74 ticks a frame), with a guest 34.5 ms (324 over 33 ms, 2.07 ticks):
physics scripts 8.5 ms a frame, process scripts 11.5, tail 5.0, draw 6.9, 1180 + 1081 draws. Even a one-tick frame
is 25-29 ms there, so the fight is not only the tick spiral. **Not done, Zach's call:** resetting the gate's
baseline to v1.29 (it would fail 1.31's release gate until phase 1 lands).

**Phase 0 as first written: measure what hurts, and make the gate bite (half a day, no game change)**
1. Reset the bar: the gate's baseline is taken once at v1.29 and then only lowered, never refreshed to last week's
   numbers. Add count budgets that
   can't flake: shadow draws, visible draws, nodes, surfaces per body, posed rigs a frame.
2. Add the stops that match the complaints: `brawl20` (20 awake grunts, guest attached), an island arrival (the
   minute after "let go"), the stronghold. Today's `fight` stop has five grunts and reads 14.7 ms: it can't see the
   problem.
3. perf.log gets the missing timers (skinning, cloth, net, move, and the frame's draw and tail) so the next play
   session attributes its own slow frames.

**Phase 2: fights (the sub-20 fps stretches)**
- Re-measure first on `brawl20` with a guest: 11-09's fixes (cached water probes, fight LOD) landed after the last
  co-op session, and the in-game tick cost 2-4x the stage's for reasons nobody has pinned down.
- Then, by what it shows: `_separation` loops all 227 enemies per thinking grunt (use the camp's list;
  `pirate_grunt.gd:1537`); `_target()` re-runs the sight check at 13 call sites a think (`:708`); ragdolls build ~15
  bodies and joints in one frame (pool them); damage numbers are a new Label3D per hit with alpha written every frame.
- A design choice for Zach: cap physics at 2 ticks a frame under overload (the game slows for a moment instead of
  the frame rate collapsing). Feel call, not a free win.

**Phase 3: stop loading the whole sea (this is 1.32's Voyage rule, built for speed first)**
- Only the island you're on and the next one exist; islands further off are one impostor mesh until ~600 m.
  Expected: nodes 42k -> ~20k, 150+ fewer enemies and their colliders in memory, tail and the far band's scripts
  (~1.9 ms of the 8.5 measured with a guest) down, shorter load.
- Big never-culled static pieces split into cells with ranges (`TownMeshes`: 33 surfaces, no range).

**Phase 4: hitches**
- A gear change rebuilds the captain's whole body on the main thread: 150-250 ms each, six in a row at minute 51 of
  the 11-07 session; a guest's change does the same to the host. Rebuild only the changed slot.
- AI captains (a rival crew of four full Player rigs) spawn in one frame: 525 and 667 ms. Navmesh at load: 8.2 s.
  Island build steps of 150-380 ms (`life #5`, crowd, colliders). Autosave reads the viewport back and encodes a PNG
  on the main thread every 120 s (`save_game.gd:585`).
- The real fix for bodies (and for the heap-corruption crash class): body meshes as data on the worker, committed
  on the main thread in slices, as the beasts already do.

**Phase 5: cheap checks, unmeasured**
- `project.godot` has `debug/settings/gdscript/always_track_call_stacks=true`, which keeps call-stack tracking on in
  release builds. A/B it in an exported build (`export_bench.ps1`).
- Retry Godot 4.8 at RC/stable (beta 2 was 6% slower here).

**Order:** phases 0 and 1 are one release's worth and need no design decisions; fighters and feature agents stay out
of bodies, shadows and world_generator meanwhile. Phase 3 is the first piece of 1.32. No new per-frame systems land
until the town stops read 12 ms.

## 3. Bugs and errors found

Confirmed by reading the code path:
1. **Loading a save drops the bounty, the bounty log, the personal sea chest and the arena rank whenever a weapon
   is in the main hand** (nearly every save). `save_game.gd:459-475`: those restores sit inside the `else` of
   `if w: player.equip_weapon(...)`. The next save then writes them empty. Since v1.19; no test round-trips them.
   **Fixed 2026-11-10** (t/items/bounty_save).
2. **The three island suites that hang on quit** (and the same shape in the game): `Humanoid._build` on the main
   thread calls `main_take_turn()` but never `main_built()` (`humanoid.gd:408`; only `:634` calls it), so during one
   long main-thread build every worker body waits ~0.5 s first, tasks are still running at quit, and the exit waits
   on a pool task that is waiting on the main thread's render sync. The game could hit it quitting or re-seeding
   mid-prepare (`world_generator.gd:74, 590`). **Fixed 2026-11-10** (`Humanoid.wait_task`, the drain at exit;
   t/island/quit_prebuild; dev_notes).
3. **The site's roadmap says "You are here" on v1.27** (`web/site/roadmap/roadmap.json` `"here": "1.27"`, not
   touched since 10-31); the latest stable is v1.30.

Found by the audit (code and logs read, not re-run):
4. One prop collider per island fails to build: Jolt "Failed to build convex polygon shape {vertex_count=1353}", 20
   times over 5 themes (`gen_island.gd:1242`: hull points pre-multiplied into island space). Something is walk-through.
5. Iron Fist plays `"punch_r"`, a pose no file defines (`power_component.gd:699`): the cast has no animation.
6. A guest's item is destroyed when the shared bag it's stored into has just gone (`network_manager.gd:2027`).
7. The stronghold vault's named legendary loses its name on reload, and renames every copy in the session
   (`stronghold_boss.gd:702`: `display_name` set on the shared cached item).
8. A wiped camp's prebuilt next crew is never released if the island unloads first (`grunt_camp.gd:169`): a bounded leak.
9. Snow islands can end up with no set piece: the cenote finds no ground in 60 tries (`kit_setpieces.gd:74`).
10. Lock-on survives sheathing, a knockout and a respawn while the camera swallows mouse-X (`targeting.gd:165`);
    maybe intended.
11. Small: beasts query the nav map before its first sync (`jungle_beast.gd:394`); `t/gear/stats.gd:81` prints a
    literal "%.2f"; two co-op tests spam "RPC '_ping' on yourself".

Clean: no script errors in the real play logs or the whole-world bench log; settings migrations, the 1.30.3 slot
rehome and the item wheel's load read fine. Still open from the handoff: decktest's rail, the rapier's balestra.

Machine: **C: has 4.5 GB free.** `tools/dev/out` is 6.8 GB, `%APPDATA%\Godot` 4.5 GB, the worktrees 1.8 GB, Temp
2.4 GB. A full system drive breaks saves, the shader cache and the page file.

## 4. What to build next

The pattern in the history: systems go wide faster than they connect. The suggestions below add little that runs
every frame, and each ties existing systems together.

1. **A performance and stability release before 1.32** (section 2, phases 0-1, plus bug 1 and 2). Every other item
   plays worse until it's done.
2. **The Voyage (1.32, already planned), with streaming as its first piece.** "Only the next island exists" is the
   biggest perf lever left and the content plan at once. Smaller, denser islands with a named cast are also cheaper.
3. **One plunder-to-upgrade loop** (Black Flag's Jackdaw, Monster Hunter's hunt-and-craft, Valheim's boss gates).
   Bosses, prizes at sea, fishing, treasure maps and the arena each pay a named material; ship lines and gear
   upgrading (the roadmap's "gear to make your own") consume them; one board at the shipwright says what the next
   upgrade needs and where it comes from. It gives gold and the unused side systems a reason, with no new world system.
4. **Crewmates you recruit** (the genre's core fantasy; Sid Meier's Pirates, Suikoden's recruits). Each island arc's
   standout character joins the ship as a cook, shipwright, navigator or doctor: a face aboard and a station bonus
   (galley buffs, slow repairs, a further log-pose read), not a fighter. Solo and duo players get a crew; cost is a
   few sleeping rigs on deck. Needs a preview first (who, what each gives).
5. **Sea Charts: replayable, star-rated contracts** (Xenoverse's Parallel Quests, Sea of Thieves' voyages), from
   `docs/inspiration_research.md` #15. Convoy raids, boss rematches with a twist, hold-the-village, bought or found
   as charts, scaled by the bounty. Post-story content from places that already exist; answers "what do I do now".
6. **Crew Rush** (Pirate Warriors' Kizuna Rush, Hades' duo boons), research #5: a gauge filled by fighting near each
   other, spent on a combination finisher by weapon family. Co-op is how the game is played, and nothing yet
   rewards fighting together.

Order suggested: 1, then 2, then 3 (with part breaks on beast bosses as its material source), then 4 or 5 by taste.
