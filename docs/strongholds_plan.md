# Strongholds and the Devil Fruits' return (Zach, 2026-10-19): concept for 1.20 or later

## What it is
Devil Fruits come back into the world properly through big set-piece places the crew has to break into:

- **Marine strongholds** (an Enies Lobby-like fortress island, run entirely by the Navy). The Navy has found a Devil
  Fruit and is guarding it. The crew sails to a heavily defended fortress and runs a **raid**: a dungeon-style event
  with waves of Marines across the fortress, a couple of stronger officers, and at the top, depending on the crew's
  level, a Vice Admiral or an **Admiral**. Win, and the fruit is yours.
- **Pirate hideouts** of the sea's great powers (Warlord or Emperor-style captains): the crew learns of a strong
  captain's hideout or island and sails there for a Devil Fruit or very significant loot. The same raid structure,
  but against a pirate crew and its captain.
- **Enemies with fruits:** the player-style AI fighters that 1.19 builds for the rival crews (captains who fight
  with the players' own weapons and skills) can also have Devil Fruit powers. An officer, an Admiral, a Warlord or
  a rival captain might be a fruit user.
- **One world pool of fruits:** each fruit exists once in the world (the devil fruits plan: one per captain,
  forever). A fruit an enemy has eaten is **taken out of the available pool**. When that enemy is killed, the fruit
  goes back into the world, and the crew can find it again later (it reappears somewhere, as fruits do in the
  source). So the fruit you saw an Admiral use can become a fruit you hunt down.

## How it fits what's planned
- docs/devil_fruits_plan.md (2026-10-15) settled why not to eat a fruit, the sea's weakness, rescues and the
  Seafarer tree, and left "drop sources and rarity" open. **This is the drop source**: the big fruits come from
  strongholds and hideouts, not from chests.
- **1.19 (the Marines + rival crews)** builds the foundations: the Marines as a faction with bounties, posters,
  patrols and named captains; the cutscene system; and AI that fights like a captain. Strongholds reuse all of it:
  Marine waves, officer fights, captain-style bosses, cutscenes before the raid and at the fruit.
- The roadmap's "Devil Fruits back before 2.0" (phase 3) and "the Marine fortress as a place" (phase 6) both land
  here.

## Open (to grill before it's built)
- **Names**: "Marine stronghold" is a placeholder. Options: Bastion, Citadel, a named fortress per sea (an Enies
  Lobby-style proper name); for the pirate side: a Warlord's hold, an Emperor's haven.
- **How the crew finds out**: rumours in the journal (1.18), a captured Marine's orders, a bounty board, the log
  pose pointing somewhere new.
- **Raid structure**: one long run or stages with checkpoints; what failing means (thrown in a cell to break out of?
  sent back to the ship?); a time limit or alarms; solo vs a full crew (scaling).
- **The Admiral fight**: when an Admiral appears (by level, by bounty), and whether you can actually win or have to
  grab the fruit and run.
- **The world pool**: how many fruits exist per save, where a freed fruit reappears (a random island, a market, a
  rumour), and what happens in co-op when two captains want the same one.
- **Enemy fruit users**: which fruits enemies may hold (from the same pool), how their powers read (the players'
  fruit moves), and whether a recurring rival can eat one between meetings.
- **Pirate hideouts**: are the Warlords and Emperors persistent named characters (like the rival crews), and does
  beating one change the sea (bounty, territory, who attacks you)?
- **Rewards besides fruits**: significant loot (legendary weapons with traits, ship parts, treasure), bounty jumps.
- **Order**: strongholds first or hideouts first, and whether the Devil Fruits' own work (the fruit list, moves,
  effects and tree polished to the weapons' level) comes before, with, or after the first stronghold.
