v1.10: The Living Isles

The islands used to be pretty. Now they're busy.

Until now, a chain island past Redtide was a gorgeous bit of jungle with a pier, a pirate camp and one very large, very cross ape. Lovely, but a bit quiet. Version 1.10 moves people in, digs some holes, grows the jungle up and gives the ape a promotion. Here's what's waiting at the end of the needle.

This is the biggest update Driftwake has had, and honestly I'm thrilled about it. It feels like a real milestone. For the first time, every island is its own little adventure, different every time you set sail, and there's enough out there to make the game properly fun to play and play again. We're nearly at the point where it all clicks together, and v1.10 is the checkpoint where you can feel it.

Pull up to a proper harbour

No more lonely plank pier. Every island's dock is now a whole harbour, and you never know which kind you'll get: a stilt port with boardwalks and huts out over the shallows, a busy wharf with warehouses and a crane, a crumbling old harbour, a bare landing on the wilder coasts, or a smugglers' cove. You'll know the cove by its dark-sailed sloop and its red lamp, which is local for "we didn't see you, you didn't see us".

Somebody actually lives here

Villages come in five flavours now: towns with lanes and a market, hamlets scattered along the paths, walled trading posts with gates and watchtowers, stilt villages built around a raised deck, and old ruins with the survivors camped nearby. Every one still has your inn, your trader, your cook and an elder with a job board, and now you can walk right into the shops and homes.

The people are the best part. Fifteen to twenty villagers keep their own hours on every island: off to work in the morning, lunch by the market, a drink at the inn when the sun goes down, bed at night. A few named locals on each island will chat, gossip about what lives in the hills and what's further down the chain, and hand you quests: find a lost sack, scout a ruin, run a parcel across town, bring them rum (it's always rum), or clear out a nest that's been bothering everyone.

Go underground (bring a torch)

Every island hides one to three caves, picked from seven kinds: bug tunnels, a smugglers' grotto, a flooded ruin, a root cavern, a bat roost, a sunken shrine and a beast's den. Each one ends in a boss and a reward, and they stay quiet until you come knocking. Some islands also have a ruined temple, with a hall full of things that bite, two levers to find and a bronze gate. There's a Warden behind it who'd rather you left. Don't.

The jungle bites back

The jungle grew up. There are six kinds of forest now (old growth, bamboo thickets, mangroves, palm fringes, strangler figs and a bit of everything), with giant canopy trees overhead and undergrowth thick enough to slow you down off the trails. Stumble off the path and you'll find landmarks too: waterfalls, step pyramids, a ship stuck in the treetops, totem groves, mines, gorge bridges. Every island also gets one big set piece, maybe a sunken village, a volcano vent, a cenote or a giant skeleton.

The Silverback got the promotion. It now fights from the canopy, bounding up into the trees, prowling from limb to limb, lobbing boulders and dropping on your head. Smack it while it's showing itself and it'll come crashing down. It also has rivals for the throne: the Strangler, a snake the length of a sloop; the Shade, a big cat that stalks you through the brush; and the Mantis Queen. An alpha boar guards the ruins, and the Thornback, a walking hill with tusks, wanders wherever it likes.

Smaller neighbours moved in as well: boars, crabs, snakes, monkeys, toucans, bats, burrowers and a few kinds of bug that jump, roll or swarm. Every island rolls its own mix, so some islands are crab heaven and some are boar country. Fireflies come out at night.

Two new pointy friends

Daggers are here, in five designs (Stiletto, Kris, Kukri, Main Gauche and Sharktooth Knife) at every tier. Fight with one or two. They're quick, close and sneaky, with crits, bleeding wounds and big bonus damage from behind. Shadow Lunge slips you round behind whoever's in front of you. Red Eclipse makes you vanish and come out behind up to six people in turn. Hundred Fangs draws a star of blinks round one unlucky pirate and then sets it off. You can also hold a dagger in a sword's off hand, reverse grip, for that extra bit of swagger.

And a few more things

  • Fights with more bite. The brood queen, Captain Grell and Captain Morrow now close the gap fast if you hang back, the canopy bugs' poison lingers on the ground, the riflemen telegraph their shove, and tougher pirates mix up their moves and dash out of the way like you do.
  • A harder road to Grell. His den on Brinehollow now sits on a clifftop out of sight of the beach, reached by a pillar-hopping climb or a rope ladder from his pier.
  • New coastlines. Sheer sea cliffs and sea stacks where the land runs high, and long sandy shelves you can wade out on everywhere else. Brinehollow got them too.
  • A comfier inventory. Weapons in your hands no longer eat bag space, and tooltips compare damage, speed and range against what you're using. It's crisper and bouncier, too.
  • Smoother arrivals. Islands build in shorter steps, so sailing up to a new one hitches less.
  • Plenty of fixes, including the option arrows that only ever went forward. Every change is in the release notes.

Come and see what your world grew

Your chain is still built from your world's seed, so your harbours, towns, caves and bosses won't match anyone else's. Round up a crew of up to four, argue about who steers, and go find out what's out there. Thank you for sailing with me this far. The best is still ahead.
