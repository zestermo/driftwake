v1.19: Wanted

You're not the only captain on the water any more.

Until now every fight in Driftwake was against grunts and beasts. That changes in this one. There are other captains out there now: rival pirate crews who want the same islands you do, and an Admiralty that would like every one of you in irons. Both fight the way you do, and both remember you.

Rival crews

The big piece of work behind this update is a new kind of enemy: a captain run by the game. Rivals aren't grunts with more health. They're built exactly like you, with the same weapons, the same moves, the same skills and the same ultimates. They watch your spacing, block and roll, punish a whiff and save their ultimate for when you leave an opening. A rookie deckhand is a lot easier to read than their captain.

Four crews sail the first chain, each with a captain of their own: the showboating Gilded Gulls, the Brine Wolves and their hammer-swinging brawler, the quiet and exact Lantern Saints, and the Mudlark Brigade, a cocky kid and their rookies who'll probably be the first crew you meet. They turn up on islands now and then, come up from the dock, and say their piece. Then it's duels, one at a time. On your own you face one or two of their mates, whoever fights most like you, and then their captain. In co-op every captain gets a rival in their own style.

The rest of the crew doesn't stand around in a ring. They wait their turn off to the side, each at their own thing: drilling with a blade, reading on a crate, tinkering, playing a fiddle or a drum, sitting on the ground, or just watching the fight. When something big happens they look up, groan or cheer, and whoever's next gets up and walks in.

Beat their captain badly enough and they call the retreat, leaving gold and sometimes a trait weapon behind. Lose and they take a cut of your gold and gloat about it. Either way they remember. The next meeting has different lines depending on who won last time, and they come back with more mates and sharper.

Cutscenes

Meetings like that needed a way to be staged, so Driftwake has a cutscene system now: camera shots, letterboxed subtitles and voice blips for every speaker. In co-op a scene only skips when everyone presses skip, so nobody misses one they wanted to watch.

A price on your head

Every captain has a bounty now, raised by what they get up to: sinking ships, beating bosses, seeing off rival crews. Each captain in a co-op crew has their own. Once it's high enough your poster goes up on the notice boards, townsfolk start to recognise you, and shops that side with the Admiralty charge you more (the shady ones charge less).

The higher it climbs, the more attention you get. Admiralty patrol ships come about when they spot you, bounty hunters wait for you ashore, and past a point a named Admiralty officer takes up your trail. There are three of them, and like the rival crews they remember how your last meeting went. An officer doesn't come alone either: they bring a squad, and they all come at you at once.

Arrested

Lose to the Admiralty and you don't wake on the beach. You wake in the brig of their ship with your gold docked and your weapons in a chest past the guards. Pick the lock, get your gear back and fight your way out. In co-op it only happens if the whole crew goes down, and then you're all in the cell together.

A new opening

A new game starts somewhere else now: a stormy night at sea, with a short story of what's out there. The wall of storm at the end of the First Sea, the Last Lantern past it, the Admiralty, the crews and the Tide Lords. Then you're a deckhand on Captain Maren Holt's ship when an Admiralty cutter runs her down. You get a blade, a fight on a rolling deck, and an order you'll carry for the rest of the game. You can skip it, of course.

A few more things in this one

  • Fighting in the water. Attack while swimming and you strike with whatever you're carrying: a quick stab, or a heavy that takes a little longer. Sharks and morays can be fought off now, and rivals will follow you in.
  • New enemy weapons. Pirates on the chain islands carry spears, warhammers, greatswords and twin axes, or fight black-leg style, each with their own reach, pace and moves. They drop what they were holding.
  • Admiralty ships are grey ironclads now, with grey-coated crews to match.

What's next

All the names and lines in this update are new, and I'll keep tuning the rivals as I play against them. Next on the roadmap is v1.20: a new island type and the first cities.

Every change is in the release notes. Watch your back.
