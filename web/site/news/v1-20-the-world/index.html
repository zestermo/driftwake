v1.20: The World

The islands got stranger, and the things living on them got better at fighting back.

This one is about the world itself. The beasts were the weakest part of the islands: copy-paste moves, stiff bodies, hard to fight up close and broken on steep ground. They've all been reworked. Every island type has a boss ground of its own now, there's a fifth kind of island, and the walk between two places has more in it than grass.

Beasts reborn

Eight beasts have new bodies, built on a rig with jointed legs that bend at rest and bodies that tilt to follow the slope under them: the Bear King, a woolly Rimeback, the Sand Lion, a long-legged Dune Strider, the armoured Thornback, a low frilled Basilisk, and two new two-legged giants, the Ice Golem and the Elder Treant. The rest kept their bodies and got polished.

All of them fight by their own move lists now. Big attacks have a wind-up you can read and an opening after, where your hits count double toward a stagger. Only one move each will knock you off your feet; the rest push you back. None of them throws the same move twice in a row, which means the Sand King no longer chains its dives, and the jungle's Strangler finally crosses its whole arena in long straight charges, like a train.

Grounds of their own

The jungle's canopy arena never suited a snowfield. Each island type has its own boss ground now. On the snow islands it's an ice grotto in the mountainside: rock walls sheathed in ice, an overhang hung with icicles, glowing crystals and something frozen in the floor. The desert's is a half-buried ruin, a sunken courtyard drowned in drifts behind broken walls. In the forest you fight inside a ring of mossy standing stones under the oldest trees.

The fog isle

The fifth island type, and the first one that's meant to scare you a little. The fog never quite lifts. Its woods are giants: trunks twisted like wrung rope, limbs that curl at the ends or snake along the ground, pale moss hanging off all of it. Down by the water there's a fishing village keeping its lamps lit, and the people there will tell you why.

The drowned hold its camps. Bullets barely slow them, they don't bleed, and when one goes down it gets back up once, unless you finish it. Ghosts are quicker, flicker in and out, and shot passes straight through them. Along the ways stand old lantern posts. Light one and the fog thins around it and the dead keep out of its glow; it stays lit for the whole crew. Its boss is the Drowned Captain, a ghost on his wreck in the bay who fights like a captain, calls his crew up out of the ground and steps out of the fog behind you.

Between places

Walking across an island used to be empty between one place and the next. Now there are fallen trunks, boulders and the stubs of old walls beside every path, and now and then a wayside shrine, a lone chest or a camp somebody left in a hurry. There are more creatures on every island and more people on the trails: travellers, hunters, ambushes.

A few more things in this one

  • Each island its own. Camps, watchtowers, gates and wells are built in their island's own roofs and walls, so there's no jungle thatch in the snow any more.
  • Driftwake's own names. Fruit powers are Driftfruits (Flux, Feral and Knack), the old fighting spirit is Will (Iron Will, Weather Eye, Captain's Will), and the sea's monsters are Leviathans. The compass that points the way down the chain is a lodestone.
  • The campfire looks like fire now, not a pale block at dusk.

What's next

The cities. The royal capital and the canal city were meant for this one, but they turned out to need a release of their own: v1.21, next on the roadmap.

Every change is in the release notes. Keep a lantern lit.
