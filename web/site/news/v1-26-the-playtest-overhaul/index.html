1.26: The Playtest Overhaul

Everything a two-hour co-op playtest turned up, worked through. And the game now updates itself.

A few days ago we sailed a crew of two from Brinehollow to level thirteen and kept a list of everything that wasn't as fun as it should be. We shipped the fixes in pieces as test builds, 1.25.1 and 1.25.2. Today it's all in, and 1.26 is the stable release.

One download that keeps up

The Download button now gets you Driftwake-Setup.exe. Run it once and the game installs with a Start menu entry; after that, new versions are offered on the title screen as a small patch, no more re-downloading the whole game. The .zip is still there if you'd rather. Want the test builds too? Turn on Get unstable updates in Options.

More from every weapon

All seventeen weapons, from the cutlass to the slingshot, grew:

  • A second ultimate. Each tree's middle branch splits: take the ultimate you know, or a new one. Put either on R, and swap between them out of a fight.
  • A second crew technique. Not another throw: set your crewmate up for a finisher, strike together from both sides, or back them up with a buff, a shield or a vanish.
  • Alternates. As mastery grows you unlock a different aerial attack, sprint attack and right-click, free, and switch them whenever you like. Every weapon has a launcher among them.
  • Aerial heavies. At mastery 10, right-click in the air is that weapon's big air move.
  • Bridges. Nodes between branches, bought from either side.

A new beginning, and the Edy tree

New captains wake on Brinehollow with nothing. Brawl on the quay with your fists, try every weapon on Sergeant Vey's rack and keep one, then climb to the shrine where Master Isao reads your Edy. The story walks you right across the island before you sail.

Your Edy now grows as one round tree of eight branches. Abilities rank up as you use them, passives can be switched off, Foresight starts dodging blows for you, and Infusion pours your Edy into every technique and blow. The auras are quieter too: haze, sparks or crackling arcs along your arms and legs, and the whole body only when you Reinforce.

Islands worth landing on

  • A villain to weaken. Every bandit camp you rout and villager you help makes the island's showdown easier.
  • Places to go in. Traders' shops you walk into, and one special place in every village. Some have a dojo, and its master will spar you for real.
  • Pirate-held villages. Fight through the lanes and over the rooftops, and free the captives, who head home.
  • At the inn. Dice and arm wrestling.

Fairer fights, a sea that pushes back

You and your enemies have poise now: fewer ragdolls, chains that actually chain, red tells on every wind-up, and cannon shots that stagger instead of flooring everyone. Bounty hunters are announced before they walk in. Admiralty cutters chase with bow guns, a blockade of three ships breaks when you sink any one of them, and your sails come down when you're rammed.

Saves without a limit

As many saves as you like, each with a picture of where you were, the version, your crew and play time, and three backups kept of every one. Host or join a crew straight from the title screen, no single-player save needed first. And in the options: Vsync, a frame cap and Reduce flashing.

What's next

1.27 starts with the cities: streets and alleys that change with every world, interiors to explore, a bell tower with a stranger who knows where the Driftfruits are, and a reason to meet the Queen. Then the Admiralty's stronghold, and the Driftfruits come back. The roadmap has it all.

Every change is in the release notes.
