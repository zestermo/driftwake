The Fog Isle

October 24, 2026

Download v1.19.3Unstable · Windows .zip

The fifth island type, and the first that wants to scare you.

  • The fog isle. Dead woods of bare oaks hung with grey moss, drowned willows over black water, a grey shingle strand, and fog so thick the next house is a shape in it. A cursed fishing village keeps its lamps lit: lamplighters, net-menders, a gravedigger, a bell-ringer.

Five new weapon kinds, a stance for every weapon, and controller support.

v1.18.2 Unstable

Every Wake a Lesson

October 20, 2026

Download v1.18.2Unstable · Windows .zip

Skills rebuilt from the ground up, new menus, and a performance pass.

  • Weapon trees. Each of the nine weapons has its own tree in three branches, and it grows only while that weapon is in your hand. Moves, techniques, the element and the ultimate come by themselves at their mastery level (1-30); stat steps are bought with a point a level, in any order. Choices are versions of the same move or passive: you keep both and switch at the ship's bunk, so nothing is ever locked away. No more mastery for weapons you aren't using.
  • Captain and Will. Your captain gets tougher every level, with milestones (Squall Step at 8, a third loadout at 10, Ballast at 14, a fourth passive slot at 25) and a talent to pick every five levels. Will comes from beating great foes, starting with Captain Morrow. Skill points are gone.
  • Adventure trees. Sailing, swimming, cooking, fishing, and new acrobatics, defense, stealth and tinkering trees, each grown by doing it. A tree appears the first time you earn it, with a card to say so.
  • One skill bar. Put any skill of a weapon in your loadouts on 1-5 or R; using one switches to that weapon and fires it. Three passive slots (a fourth later) and a library of everything you know, in the new Loadout tab.
  • A new look. The skills menu (K) has Captain, Combat, Adventure and Loadout tabs with node trees; menus, dialogue, the HUD's signs and the radial wheels are redone in a hand-made seafaring style.
  • Performance. Islands are built off the main thread and let go a little at a time, effects warm up behind the loading card, occlusion in towns, and many smaller savings: fewer hitches arriving at islands and starting fights.
  • Old saves. Mastery levels are kept and their points come back.

v1.19

Wanted

October 23, 2026

Rival crews, the Admiralty and a price on your head, plus everything from the v1.18.1-1.18.4 unstable builds. Read the post.

  • Rival captains. Captains run by the game, built like yours: the same weapons, moves, skills and ultimates. They read your spacing, block, roll and parry some of the time, punish whiffs and save their ultimate for an opening.
  • Rival crews. The Gilded Gulls, the Brine Wolves, the Lantern Saints and the Mudlark Brigade turn up on chain islands: a meeting, then duels one at a time. Alone you face one or two of their best matches for your style, then their captain; in co-op each captain gets a rival in their own style. The rest of the crew waits off to the side, drilling, reading, tinkering, playing an instrument, sitting or watching, and looks up when something big happens. A knockdown is never a loss, only being beaten. Beat their captain for gold and maybe a trait weapon; lose and they take a cut of yours. They remember the result and come back stronger.
  • Cutscenes. Camera shots with letterboxed subtitles and voice blips; in co-op a scene skips once everyone presses skip.
  • Bounties. Each captain's own, raised by sinking ships, beating bosses and rivals. Wanted, Notorious, Infamous and Dreaded: posters on notice boards, townsfolk who know your face, Admiralty-minded shops that charge more or turn you away, and shady ones that charge less.
  • The Admiralty. Grey ironclad patrol ships that come about for a wanted captain, bounty hunters ashore, and three named officers with squads who remember you and track you down the chain.
  • Arrest and escape. Lose to the Admiralty (the whole crew, in co-op) and you wake in their brig: gold docked, weapons in a chest past the guards. Pick the lock and fight your way out.
  • A new opening. A narrated storm at sea sets up the world, then a short fight on Captain Maren Holt's deck when an Admiralty cutter runs her down. Skippable.
  • Fighting in the water. Attack while swimming to strike with your own weapon; sharks and morays can be fought, and rivals follow you in.
  • New enemy weapons. Spear, warhammer, greatsword, twin-axe and black-leg pirates on the chain islands, each with its own reach, pace and moves; they drop their weapon.
  • Performance. Cheaper character bodies and combat effects.

v1.18

Charted Waters

October 19, 2026

Everything you do makes you better at it: reworked skills, a real economy, food and fishing, diving, a map you hold and a journal for the crew. Read the post.

  • Skills, rebuilt. Fewer, bigger nodes that change how you play. A shared Captain tree on level points, weapon trees that fill as you fight with them, and weapon families that share a quarter of the mastery you earn. Old captains get every point back once.
  • Life skills. Swimming, Seamanship, Cooking and Fishing level up by doing them, with perks at milestones.
  • Loot and gold. Much scarcer drops, rolled for each captain in co-op. One price curve for everything you buy and sell.
  • Weapon traits. From blue rarity up, every weapon has a named trait that changes a move: a bleeding finisher, a fire-trail roll, a riposte on a clean parry, a crit right after a roll, and seven more.
  • Shops. Each shop is a trade with staples and a stock that changes every day. Rare gear only at treasure merchants. Buy back what you just sold. Artisans camp on many islands' trails.
  • Food and healing. Health comes back slowly after a fight. In a fight, eat and drink from the quick item (E; hold for the wheel). Cook ingredients in the galley or at a campfire into meals with 10-20 minute buffs. The galley no longer restocks food for free.
  • Fishing. Four rods to buy, four kinds of bait, a cast-wait-strike minigame with the bobber on the real waves, and 21 catches from common to legendary by water, island and time of day. Your first legendary teaches a recipe.
  • Diving and the sea. Dive with a breath meter for sunken chests, oyster beds and reefs. Rip currents, surf, storm swell and cold water near snow islands. Sharks, morays and jellyfish. New strokes and climbing out onto rocks. A tired swimmer floats instead of drowning.
  • The held map. M unrolls a parchment in your hands. Fog over what the crew hasn't explored, question marks near undiscovered places, and a zoom out to the sea chart.
  • Quests and people. A shared crew journal (J), notice boards, quests with several steps handed back to whoever gave them, rewards of gold, items, recipes, discounts and charts. Villagers say their own lines, and ten kinds of encounters turn up on the islands.
  • Downtime at sea. Lash the helm and train, read, tinker, fish or sleep while the ship holds her course, until something interrupts.
  • Controls. Abilities on 1-5, weapon loadouts on X, an action wheel on N (the same ashore and aboard), making camp, an equipment slot with a show/hide box, crew chat (Enter), shift+click to move stacks, and pickups never equip themselves.
  • Ships. Swinging ropes are a yard upgrade; the rope wins the prompt mid-jump and swings clear of the guns; enemy crews board you.
  • Performance. Towns run smoother and the stutter when the next island loads in is smaller.
  • Fixes. The sprint jump costs stamina, the stern's corners are closed, and a long list of smaller fixes.

v1.17

Timber and Tide

October 17, 2026

Forest islands join the chain, the sloop becomes a ship you build up, and Brinehollow grows into a real town. Read the post.

  • Forest islands. Autumn woodland: maples, oaks, beeches, birches and firs, rolling hills, and rivers that fall into gorges with a pool at the foot. A lumber town on its river with a water-wheel sawmill, a log flume, charcoal camps and a hunter's lodge. A waterfall gorge with a cave and rope bridges, a ruined castle with a keep to climb, and a hollow giant tree with a root cavern.
  • Forest wildlife and bosses. Red deer herds (the stag charges), foxes, will-o'-wisps, wood sprites and walking stumps. Bosses: the Stag King and the Elder Treant, with the moss golem, the Cursed Huntsman and his hounds, and the Wild Hunt abroad at night. Its own day and night music and a woodland ambience.
  • Ship upgrades. Tackett's refits are twelve upgrade lines with levels, each one visible on the sloop: hull plating, the gun deck, sails, rudder, copper bottom, a bow chaser, a crow's nest lookout, lanterns, railings, a tangerine grove, galley and bunks, and the hold. Higher levels need salvage (timber, iron, canvas, copper), floating from ships you sink and in wrecks and chests. In co-op anyone can chip in. Old refits carry over.
  • Seamanship. A SEA skill tree for each captain, earned by sailing, gunnery, repairs and the pump, with bonuses at the station you're manning.
  • Brinehollow, bigger. The island grew south with farmland, a windmill hamlet, a waterfall pool, a wreck and a second cove. A market street of shopfronts, a second row of houses on the hill, a shrine terrace with a pagoda at the top of a long stair, and woods all round the town.
  • Brinehollow, livelier. Shopkeepers on daily routines, the Salted Gull busy at night, lanterns strung across the market street, chimney smoke, market and tavern sound, fishing boats and dockers at the harbour. Haldor's Forge sells the weapons, Corin sells instruments, Vey sells armour.
  • Doors and steps. Every building has a proper door and steps you can walk up, the lighthouse and harbour tower included. Roofs lost their dark seam and buried chimneys.
  • The katana. The combo, the air slash and all five skills follow the blade's real path, with drawn steel slashes, a steel-blue cloud and sakura awakening, and a petal aura on the blade.
  • Fixes. Co-op guests' clocks settle right after joining, so the waves no longer run out of step for the first seconds.

v1.16

Sand and Snow

October 17, 2026

The island chain goes past the jungle: it now rolls jungle, snow and desert islands, each with its own land, towns, people, wildlife, bosses, weather and music. Read the post.

  • Snow islands. A tall peak with pointed spires, frozen lakes and a frozen river, and a castle on the summit of most. Round pink-brick villages under tiered green roofs, snowy fences and a tame shagback by the market. Ice-block ruins, a town buried in the drifts, a ship frozen into the pack ice, and ice caves with icicles, glowing crystals and a frozen waterfall.
  • Snow wildlife and bosses. Wolves hunt in packs, white bears charge, walrus bulls guard their colonies, hares bolt and shagback herds stampede when you hit one. Bosses: the White Shade, the Bear King and the Ice Golem, with Frostfang at the ruins and the Rimeback roaming.
  • Desert islands. The biggest islands yet: plateaus, mesas, dune fields, dry riverbeds, canyons, salt pans and oases. Pastel flat-roofed towns with domes and awnings, packed medinas with minarets and a great domed hall, houses, a caravanserai and a potter's workshop you can walk into. Caravan camps, an obelisk, twin seated guardians, a ruined skyline, a buried city and a tomb in the cliffs.
  • Desert wildlife and bosses. Scorpions, monitor lizards, fennec foxes and circling vultures. Bosses: the Sand King (a sandworm that hunts from under the sand), the Dune Emperor, the Basilisk and the Sand Lion, plus a hyena alpha, the Dune Strider and a bandit chief running the pirates' camp.
  • Weather to match. Snow islands sit under a white haze with snow always falling, heavy spells and the odd blizzard. Deserts never rain: sandstorms roll in instead and the lamps come on.
  • Footprints. Boot prints in snow and sand that slowly fill back in, a soft snow layer your boots sink into, snow and sand kicked up as you walk, snow and ice footsteps, and your breath in the cold. Footsteps on every generated island now sound like the ground you're on.
  • New music. Desert and snow tracks for the wilds and the towns, each with a night version, plus wind, sandstorm and blizzard ambience.
  • Hand-drawn effects. Smoke, dust, sand, snow, mist and blood all use a new library of sprite effects, and fire and explosions throw a brief flash of light. Kraken's Wake, the cutlass's ultimate, now pours out flowing ink-drawn water, and with Call of the Tide learned, water winds round your drawn cutlass.
  • Capes and the cloak. Capes are a tattered trapezoid with painted folds and stay off your legs. Sela sells a heavy cloak. The belt lantern hangs at your hip.
  • Fixes. Minibosses now turn up on every kind of island, and a few chains that couldn't name their islands now can.

v1.15

Cliffs and Currents

October 15, 2026

  • The cliff path to Grell. The way up to Grell's den is now a ledge cut into the sea cliff: about twice as long, with cable railings, moss and overhangs, five gaps that drop to the sea (swim back to the sand ramp and try again), a bug cave with a tough cave brute and poison bugs on the ceiling, and a broken bridge you cross on a hanging chain. Grell waits by his fire until you get close.
  • The pier ladder. Grell's pier ladder stays rolled up until someone reaches the top the hard way, then it's a shortcut for good, for the whole crew.
  • Water that knows the shore. Waves calm over the shallows and roll in along the beach instead of across it, sheltered coasts are calmer, and the sea never comes up through the sand any more.
  • Smoke and fire. Cannons leave a bank of powder smoke, a ball on a hull bursts into a fireball, cannonballs throw up plumes of water, and heavy hits kick up real dust clouds.
  • Swings keep your speed. Letting go of a vine, a ship's rope or a chain keeps your momentum.
  • Steadier timing. Sprint shake, the marker cooldown, thunder, villager barks and the weather run on game time, so a hitch can't throw them off.

v1.14

Smooth Sailing

October 15, 2026

The performance update. Read the story behind it.

  • Far-off things nap. Pirates, bugs, enemy ships and villagers more than about 160 m from every captain stop thinking, moving and animating, and wake up within about 120 m exactly where they were (villagers back on their routine), in co-op too.
  • Less to draw. Characters are drawn in about 15 pieces instead of 23, small props fade out at a distance and stop casting shadows, sun shadows reach 80 m, and fog banks are drawn as one.
  • Calmer physics. Hitboxes and interaction spots stopped constantly checking for overlaps (6 of 720 still do), moored ships rest until you come near, and a slow frame can't snowball into more physics catch-up.
  • Smarter background work. The weather, birds, clouds, shore sounds, spray and puddles update a little less often, and the closed inventory screen stopped running in the background.
  • The result. Brinehollow on the dev laptop: about 24 → 108 fps at midday, 28 → 107 at night, 31 → 99 in rain, with the same look. F8 now shows enemies awake / asleep.
  • Stances and parries. Lower, wider twin daggers; one dagger's off hand low at your side; dual swords lower; each with its own block, parry and parry flourish.
  • Fixes. Arms no longer stretch after knockdowns or with hand-placed weapons and instruments; combos, reloads and buffered inputs count in game time; nothing kills a duelist mid-duel.

v1.13

Shore Leave

October 15, 2026

Things to do with your crew when nobody's trying to sink you.

  • Instruments. A tin whistle, a bodhrán, a concertina, a fiddle and a hurdy-gurdy. Gus at The Salted Gull sells the first three; the fiddle and hurdy-gurdy turn up with chain-island traders. Nobody starts with one. Wear it in the new INST slot and hold T to play.
  • Jam with your crew. Four original shanties (a jig, a hauling shanty, a waltz and a polka), each with a part for every instrument. Start playing near a crewmate who's already at it and you come in on their tune at the same spot, so a whole crew plays one song. The music dips while anyone nearby plays.
  • Duels. Walk up to a crewmate and press F to trade, inspect their gear, or challenge them. Their health goes up top like a boss's, only the two of you can land a blow, the loser just gets knocked down (nothing lost), you both stand up healed, and the winner gets a little XP. Walk away and it's a draw.
  • Hits you can hear. Blades slice, axes chop, fists thud, shots smack, and parries and blocks ring steel on steel. Heavy hits and finishers sound heavier, for every attack, technique and ultimate, yours and the enemies'.
  • Menus that click. Hover, confirm, back, a "can't do that", opening and closing screens, equip, buy and sell, level up, and quests taken and finished.
  • Fixes. The waves no longer jump after a hitch; the hornpipe and shanty emotes leave the music to the instruments.

v1.12

Night Watch

October 14, 2026

Light, music and a living sea: the world after dark, and how it sounds.

  • Nights you can see in. Moonlit blue nights instead of black ones, with a floor so storms never go pitch dark, plus more lamps, lit windows and doorways across Brinehollow, the villages and harbours, and lanterns at the den and the camps.
  • The belt lantern. Every captain wears a brass lantern at the hip. It lights itself at dusk, swings as you run and stop, goes out when you swim, and L toggles it. Your crew sees yours.
  • Lanterns at sea. Your ship has six: the stern corners, the mast, the bow, the cabin door and the rails. Pirate ships carry two, and you can spot them from far off at night.
  • New music. A new title theme, and 2 to 2.5 minute pieces for the open sea, the island wilds and the villages, each with a sparser night version that fades in on the same bar. The music dips under cannons and roars.
  • A living sea. A bow wave that grows with speed, slaps and sheets of spray when she meets a wave, mist across the deck in a blow, surf breaking differently on beaches, rocks and quays, spindrift off the crests, drifting foam and rings where things splash.
  • Ocean sound. A deep swell, lapping close by, far breakers, rock crashes and wind gusts, all by distance to the real coast, and everything muffled when you dive.
  • Birds. Gulls round the harbours (perched on roofs and posts until you run at them), gulls that follow your ship near land, high flocks over the islands and parrots in the jungle. Fewer at dusk, none at night or in a storm.
  • Rain that lands. Dimmer, scene-lit rain that stops and splashes on the ground, roofs and decks, puddles that build up and dry out, and rings on the sea.
  • Longer days. Daylight now lasts 14 minutes and the night 10, in the same 24-minute day.
  • Small things. Pixelation is one on/off option (Options > Video, or F2) at the sharper setting; the coarser ones are gone. The lodestone moved to H.

v1.11

Sea Legs

October 13, 2026

The feel update: how your captain moves, rests, sneaks, heals and shows off.

  • New moves. Standing, running and sprinting jumps with their own take-offs and landings, a somersaulting double jump, a snappier dash, and the lodestone (L) held up properly.
  • Draw and sheathe. Every weapon kind draws and puts away its own way, with 13 new sounds; axes now hang on your back.
  • Healing in a fight. Food, rum, stew and the new Sea Tonic (55, sold by Nessa, Ida and chain traders) share an 8 s cooldown and take a moment to eat or drink; a hit, roll or knockdown before it lands spills it.
  • Sitting. Sit on benches, stools, logs, crates and Morrow's chair (F), or on the ground from the emote wheel; ship seats sail with you; one captain per seat in co-op.
  • Sneaking. C crouches (X in the browser): quieter steps, and grunts and creatures notice you at half range, much less from behind.
  • Emotes. Hold T for a wheel of twelve: wave, point, salute, laugh, cheer, hornpipe, sit, shrug, bow, flex, facepalm and a shanty sway, with seven new sounds, seen by your whole crew.
  • Gestures. Captains and villagers gesture as they talk; chests open with both hands, levers are hauled, loot is picked up and items are handed over at trades and turn-ins.
  • More drip, less clipping. Coats, vests, belts, armour, collars and boots sit properly over each other, and nine new pieces: slouch hat, boarding helm, striped shirt, leather harness, admiral's coat, slops, cavalier boots, sword baldric and a spyglass.
  • Every screen, crisp. The skill map (tree summaries, hover tooltips, what a node opens), shops on the inventory's slots with compare arrows, dialogue planks, the chart, the yard, save slots, the creator and the HUD (key-cap prompts, a gilt boss bar) all draw at a whole-number scale; the pause menu is in two columns.
  • Fixes. A save made in the first moments after loading could send you back to the dock; the character creator compiles again in tests.

v1.10

The Living Isles

October 13, 2026 · Read the announcement

The biggest update yet, and a milestone: every island is now its own adventure, and there's enough out there for a properly fun, replayable game.

  • Fights with more bite. The brood queen, Captain Grell and Captain Morrow get gap closers with clear tells, the canopy bugs' and the queen's poison leaves puddles that poison you, the riflemen's shove has a wind-up you can read, and pirates have more moves, flank in groups, and the tougher ones dash out of the way.
  • New drip. 23 new pieces of clothing and armour: kettle helm, fez, headband, crown, mail coif, doublet, kimono, leather jerkin, padded jack, scale vest, haori, duster, frock coat, leather bracers, long gloves, hakama, leather trousers, sandals, sabatons, a wide belt, a tooth necklace, brass goggles and a fur mantle. Sela sells the cloth and leather, Vey the steel, and the crown is plunder only.
  • Grell's den moves up. On Brinehollow it now sits on a clifftop plateau out of sight of the wake-up beach, behind a rocky ridge, reached by a pillar-hopping climb along a knife-edge ledge or a rope ladder from its pier.
  • Harbours. Every chain island's pier is now a whole harbour district, in one of five kinds: a stilt port with boardwalks and huts out over the water, a smugglers' cove with a dark-sailed sloop and a red signal lamp, a busy wharf with warehouses and a crane, a crumbling old harbour, or a bare landing on the wilder coasts.
  • Villages, five ways. Towns with lanes and a market, hamlets spread out along the paths, walled trading posts with gates and watchtowers, stilt villages round a raised deck, and old ruins with the survivors camped nearby. Every one has an inn, a trader, a cook, an elder and a job board, and you can walk into the shops and homes.
  • Villagers with lives. Fifteen to twenty people on each island who go to work, break for lunch, gather at the inn or the plaza at dusk and head home at night. Three named locals on every island have their own talk, rumours about the island and the chain ahead, and quests: lost things to find, places to scout, parcels to deliver, goods to fetch and nests to clear.
  • Caves, dungeons and the ruined temple. One to three caves on every island from seven kinds (bug tunnels, a smugglers' grotto, a flooded ruin, a root cavern, a bat roost, a sunken shrine and a beast's den), each ending in a boss and a reward. Some islands also hide a ruined temple: two levers, a bronze gate and the Warden behind it.
  • The jungle, grown up. Six kinds of forest (mixed, old growth, bamboo thicket, mangrove coast, palm fringe and strangler figs), giant canopy trees and thick undergrowth that slows you down off the trails. Huts, piers and camps are built in the jungle's own style, and the jungle has its own music, ambience and weather.
  • Landmarks and set pieces. Waterfalls, wrecks, great trees, step pyramids, watchtowers, a wreck stuck in the treetops, totem groves, hermits' huts, mines, aqueducts and gorge bridges. Each island also gets one set piece, from a sunken village or a volcano vent to a giant skeleton, a sea arch, hot springs, a cenote or an old fort.
  • Jungle bosses. The Silverback now takes to the canopy: it prowls from limb to limb, hurls boulders from the branches and drops on you from above. Hit it while it shows itself and you'll knock it out of the tree. Three new bosses share its throne: the Strangler (a giant snake), the Shade (a big cat that stalks you through the brush) and the Mantis Queen. There are minibosses too: an alpha boar at the ruins and the Thornback, a huge beast that roams the island.
  • Jungle creatures. Boars, leaping and rolling bugs, swarming mites, burrowers, bats, toucans, monkeys, snakes and crabs. Each island rolls its own mix, with fireflies and butterflies on top.
  • New coastlines. Jagged sea cliffs and sea stacks where the land meets the sea up high, and long sandy shelves you can wade out on everywhere else. Brinehollow got them too.
  • Daggers. A new weapon in five designs (Stiletto, Kris, Kukri, Main Gauche and Sharktooth Knife) at every tier. Fight with one dagger or two: each set has its own combo, heavy attack (Shadow Lunge), jump attack, skill tree and ultimate (Red Eclipse and Hundred Fangs). Expect crits, bleeding wounds and backstabs. A dagger also fits in a sword's off hand, held in a reverse grip.
  • Inventory refresh. A cleaner, crisper inventory. Weapons in your hands no longer take up bag space, and tooltips show damage, speed and range with a comparison against what you're using. Everything pops and bounces a little more, too.
  • Fixes. The < > option selectors now step back as well as forward. Islands load more smoothly because their building is spread over shorter steps. Co-op fixes for ruined villages and for crewmates at the helm.

v1.9

Beasts and Blades

October 12, 2026

  • The beasts rebuilt. The Silverback is a real gorilla now, and the Leviathan is a long serpent that circles your ship, rears over the deck and bites.
  • Sword and pistol. The Corsair style puts a blade and a pistol in one combo, with Powder Lunge, Dead Man's Drop, Powder Finale, a butt-strike while you reload, and its own skill tree with an ultimate.
  • Dual wield. Fixed and polished. Pair a sword with a second sword, an axe or a dagger: the off hand changes the weight of each hit, and Twin Spin is in the tree.
  • Driftfruits rest for now. Chests no longer hold them while their powers are reworked. Fruits you've already eaten stay yours.
  • Co-op. A pistol shot fired across your own body no longer vanishes, and a guest who reaches an island that's still building keeps its fights.
  • Brinehollow as a kit. The starter island is built from reusable pieces, the first step to giving every chain island its level of detail.

v1.8

The Chain

October 9, 2026

  • The island chain. Past Redtide, the lodestone leads to islands generated from your world's seed. Jungle isles have a pirate camp and its named captain, a bug lair, ruins and a summit lookout, and each one is tougher than the last (enemies scale with the island's level).
  • Outpost villages. An inn, a trader and a cook, a job board and an elder on every island.
  • The Silverback. Each jungle isle's beast, with its hoard and the Silverback Pelt.
  • Reading the lodestone. Needles on the compass, each island's theme and danger, forks you pick by sailing, and a chart that follows you along the chain. The needle settles after 15 minutes on an island, or as soon as its beast falls.
  • The sea between. Pirate fleets at the next island's level, hazards, Leviathans, wrecks, bottles and islets on every leg.
  • Combat. The katana and the axe reworked (Thousand Petals, Maelstrom, Earthsplitter), and jumping attacks for every weapon.
  • Sound. Layered hull wash that follows your speed (no more short loop), footsteps by surface, varied hits and shots, and island music that resumes after a fight.
  • Pacing. Slower levelling, weaker long-range pistols, faster and fiercer pirate ships.
  • Co-op. Guests see boarders, and late-built islands catch up on fights.
  • In the browser at driftwake.fun (co-op stays on desktop).

v1.7

Wind and Story

October 8, 2026

  • A story to start. Wake on the beach past the quay and follow Old Pell's lead through Brinehollow to Captain Morrow, with your next goal on screen and a marker to follow.
  • Brinehollow grows. A deep western forest, Captain Grell's pirate den, spitters in the canopy and the Brood Queen's cave.
  • The harbour town. A stone quay with piers, trading ships and a harbour tower. The village gets dressed houses, a furnished tavern, a cobbled plaza, a smithy and a sea chapel.
  • Morrow's lodestone. Hold L to see where the islands of the chain lie.
  • Combat. A new cutlass combo and polished techniques (the Kraken's Wake), elements woken by weapon mastery, and heavy attacks that tire you when spammed.
  • Fairer sea fights. Enemy hull bars, far fewer holes, faster pumping, and ships that ram or board up close instead of firing. The wind only ever helps.
  • Redtide Rock. The fort's guns are gone; a stone stair, sea stacks and crags instead.
  • Sound and sight of the sea. Surf, swell, hull wash, creaking timbers, gusting wind and whistling rigging; surf rolling in, whitecaps, wind streaks and spray.
  • Climbing by hand, and interaction prompts only when you're right next to things.
