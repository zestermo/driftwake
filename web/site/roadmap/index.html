Roadmap

Where the ship's headed. Plans change at sea, so treat this as a heading, not a promise.

Next up: the economy and skills

v1.13 gave the crew something to do ashore: instruments that jam together and duels (see what shipped). Next, what you earn and spend.

  • A fairer economy. Gold that means something, shops worth visiting, and treasure you actually want to keep: rare-gear merchants who only take treasure, and quests and crafting that use it.
  • Skills that grow evenly. A clearer skill map, even pacing across weapons, and a balance pass so every style has its moment.

Islands that feel planned, not rolled

  • District planning. Each island's harbour, village, outskirts and wilds laid out like somewhere people actually built, with a big set piece and a beast's ground on every one.
  • Smoother sailing. The islands ahead stay light until you get close, so arriving never hitches.
  • New biomes. Snowy isles, desert islands and temperate forests, each with its own towns, creatures, bosses and music, so a chain changes as you sail it.

Devil Fruits: coming back, done right

Devil Fruits are the heart of the kind of adventure Driftwake is chasing, which is exactly why they're on the shelf for now. A fruit changes everything about how you fight, move and survive, and it should feel that big. So before putting something that important into the world, I wanted the core of the game properly solid first: movement, combat, the islands, the sea and co-op.

When they come back, each fruit gets the same care as the weapons: its own moves, effects and skill tree, real trade-offs (the sea won't be your friend), and how fruit users play together in co-op, including a crewmate diving in to haul a sinking fruit user out of the water.

Marines, bounties and being hunted

Right now the sea's trouble is other pirates. Later, it's the law.

  • Wanted posters. Everything you sink, raid and beat adds to your bounty. Your poster goes up in towns, and the number on it keeps climbing.
  • Marine patrols. Marine ships sail the busier waters. Get spotted with a price on your head and they'll come about.
  • The hunt. Once your bounty is high enough, named Marine captains start hunting you personally, tracking your ship down the chain, waiting at harbours and showing up when you least want them.
  • Bigger bounty, bigger trouble. The higher it goes, the tougher the people who come for you. Eventually that means the Marines' best.

City arenas

Duels and shanties shipped in v1.13. Next for the crew:

  • City arenas. Nonlethal tournaments with wooden versions of every weapon, a big crowd, and round after round: one-on-one, team battles where your colours pick your side, and free-for-alls. Enter on your own, or against your own crew.

Further out

  • More weapons. Twin axes, a two-handed hammer, and ability tiers where a mastered skill really changes how it works: a dodge that shadow-steps, Observation Haki that sidesteps for you.
  • More minibosses. A pirate warlord holding a camp, a bat swarm-mother, a carnivorous plant the size of a house.
